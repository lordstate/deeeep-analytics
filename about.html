Rules & methodology

How the tournaments and the rating work.

Format

Every Deeeep.io 1v1 Tournament is played as a single-elimination bracket — lose once and you're out. Every match is Best of 5 (first to 3 game wins), so a match can end 3-0, 3-1, or 3-2. There are no group stages and no third-place playoff.

The three editions

  • 2023 — the first edition, around 38 players.
  • 2024 — grew to over 70 players, with an added Qualifications round.
  • 2026 — the biggest Deeeep.io 1v1 tournament to date, with 256 players.

The Elo rating system

Ratings follow the classic chess Elo formula. Every player starts at 800. After each match, both players' ratings shift based on the result and on how surprising it was:

  • Beating a higher-rated opponent gains you more points than beating a lower-rated one.
  • The rating change uses a fixed K-factor of 32 for every player, regardless of experience.
  • The scoreline (3-0 vs 3-2) has no effect on the rating change — a win is a win.
  • There are no draws, so every match produces a clear rating shift.
  • Byes don't affect anyone's rating.

Ratings carry over between editions: your 2024 starting rating is exactly your rating at the end of 2023 (or 800 if it's your first tournament). Matches are replayed in strict chronological order — 2023 first, then 2024, then 2026, round by round — so the whole rating history is a faithful reconstruction of how the community's skill picture evolved.

To keep upsets from being too punishing — and to add a touch of gentle rating inflation over time, similar to Clash Royale's trophy system — losers only lose 90% of the standard Elo calculation, while winners still gain the full amount.

Round dates

Exact per-match timestamps aren't available from the bracket exports, so round dates shown on the site are estimated: each tournament is assumed to start in the summer of its year, with one round played per week.

Sources
